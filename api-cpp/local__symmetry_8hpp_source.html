69// A bond is counted eclipsed when its azimuthal cosine exceeds this value.
70// Both bonds are projected onto the plane perpendicular to the central bond.
71// Ideal eclipsed is cos 0 = 1 and ideal staggered is cos 60 degrees = 0.5.
72// The ideal-cell fast path does not use this cosine. It uses the raw dot
73// product of the second-shell bond with another first-shell bond, which is
74// 1/3 staggered and 7/9 eclipsed, and it compares the square of that number.
75// At 220-270 K the staggered azimuthal cosines of mW ice reach about 0.85
76// while the eclipsed bonds stay at 1, so 0.90 is the gap the second-shell
77// template leaves between them. 0.75 counts those warmed staggered bonds
78// as eclipsed.
79inline constexpr double kEclipsedCosine = 0.90;
80
81// Scale-free ceiling on the second-shell residual. Both clouds are centered,
82// then the RMSD is taken after the optimal rotation and the optimal scale
83// and divided by the template's root-mean-square radius (Horn 1987; Larsen,
84// Schmidt, Schiotz 2016). Zero is a perfect shape. 0.12 is the quenched mW
85// operating point. A TIP4P/2005 shell at the melting point still vibrates:
86// raising the ceiling from 0.12 to 0.15 recovers quench-confirmed crystal,
87// and the next step to 0.18 mostly adds atoms that neither PTM nor CHILL+
88// call ice after the same minimisation.
89inline constexpr double kShellRmsd = 0.15;
