66// A bond is counted eclipsed when its azimuthal cosine exceeds this value.
67// Both bonds are projected onto the plane perpendicular to the central bond.
68// Ideal eclipsed is cos 0 = 1 and ideal staggered is cos 60 degrees = 0.5.
69// The ideal-cell fast path does not use this cosine. It uses the raw dot
70// product of the second-shell bond with another first-shell bond, which is
71// 1/3 staggered and 7/9 eclipsed, and it compares the square of that number.
72// At 220-270 K the staggered azimuthal cosines of mW ice reach about 0.85
73// while the eclipsed bonds stay at 1, so 0.90 is the gap the second-shell
74// template leaves between them. 0.75 counts those warmed staggered bonds
75// as eclipsed.
76inline constexpr double kEclipsedCosine = 0.90;
77
78// Scale-free ceiling on the second-shell residual. Both clouds are centred,
79// then the RMSD is taken after the optimal rotation and the optimal scale
80// and divided by the template's root-mean-square radius (Horn 1987; Larsen,
81// Schmidt, Schiotz 2016). Zero is a perfect shape. The value is the operating
82// point chosen on seeds 91001 and 92002. A thermal label is scored against the
83// majority of this kernel, CHILL+ and PTM on the steepest-descent quench.
84// 0.12 is the tightest ceiling whose recall on those two seeds stays above
85// PTM when a crystal atom must have one crystal neighbour. Precision on that
86// pair is 0.994, against PTM at 0.995. Seed 93003 was not used to choose it.
87inline constexpr double kShellRmsd = 0.12;
