Detailed Description

Triclinic fractional density, pore-frame profiles, and host-guest contacts.

A LAMMPS cell has edge vectors a, b, c recovered from the dump box. Fractional coordinates live in the unit cube. A slab of fractional thickness 1/n along a cell edge has volume V/n, with V = det(H). An arbitrary Cartesian direction bins the projection of that cube. Its bin volumes are the exact slice measures of the unit cube, times V, so a uniform gas is flat at N/V even when the projected slabs are not equally thick in the projection coordinate's density of states.

Cylindrical shells are Euclidean distances to a pore axis parallel to a cell edge. The radial coordinate is the distance to the nearest periodic image of that axis. Within the inscribed radius of the centred perpendicular face the nearest image is the face origin. Beyond it, a corner of the centred face can lie closer to a neighbouring image, and the coordinate follows that image. The shell volume is the area of the annulus inside the Voronoi cell of the perpendicular lattice, times the length of the edge, so a uniform fluid stays flat. The azimuth is the angle in the centred face, and its sector is clipped to that face.
