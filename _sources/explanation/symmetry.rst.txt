==============
Local symmetry
==============


The classifier names each oxygen as ice Ih, ice Ic, stacking-disordered
ice, or unmatched, and it can name a hydrate cage or a five- or six-ring
when one cluster is passed in. The per-atom ice label is the production
path. The command and the per-atom call are on
`the symmetry classifier <../user/howto/symmetry-classifier.rst>`_.

A crystal oxygen is ice Ih (0), ice Ic (1), or stacking-disordered ice
(2). Anything else is -1. The seventeen-atom shell is the central
oxygen, its four nearest neighbours, and the twelve neighbours of those
four. That is the shell polyhedral template matching uses for diamond
(Larsen, Schmidt and Schiøtz, *Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering* 24, 055007, 2016,
DOI 10.1088/0965-0393/24/5/055007,
`arXiv:1603.05143 <https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05143>`_).

Neighbours
----------

The bond cutoff is 3.5 Å. A neighbour closer than 2.15 Å is dropped.
The four nearest bonds are kept. One thread walks each pair once, using
half of the 27-cell stencil, and writes both ends of the bond. Several
threads scan the full stencil and write one end. When the cell is
orthorhombic and every edge is at least three bond lengths, the atoms
are wrapped into the box and histogrammed into cells of that cutoff.
A skewed cell uses a wider stencil. The coordinate columns are reused
on the next frame of the same size.

A center whose four bonds do not all lie between 2.30 Å and 3.35 Å is
left unmatched, and the second shell is not read.

Tetrahedral order
-----------------

The tetrahedral order parameter :math:`q` is the Chau–Hardwick /
Errington–Debenedetti invariant of the four bonds (Errington and
Debenedetti, *Nature* 409, 318, 2001,
`10.1038/35053024 <https://doi.org/10.1038/35053024>`_). With
:math:`\theta_{jk}` the angle at the center between neighbours :math:`j` and
:math:`k`,



.. math::

    q = 1 - \frac{3}{8}
    \sum_{j=1}^{3} \sum_{k=j+1}^{4}
    \left(\cos\theta_{jk} + \frac{1}{3}\right)^{2}.

Equal bonds skip this test. An ideal crystal has equal bonds, and the
staggered test below names it. Otherwise a center with :math:`q < 0.85` is
left unmatched and the second shell is not read. The library constant
is ``kThermalQ``.

Common-neighbour fingerprint
----------------------------

The second shell is the adaptive-cutoff common-neighbour signature of
Maras, Trushin, Stukowski, Albe and Nordlund, *Computer Physics Communications* 205, 13 (2016), Appendix A
(`10.1016/j.cpc.2016.04.001 <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2016.04.001>`_).
The cutoff for a bond of length :math:`r` is :math:`r(1+\sqrt{2})/2`.

Twelve :math:`(4,2,1)` patterns are ice Ic. Six :math:`(4,2,1)` and six
:math:`(4,2,2)` are ice Ih. A partial cubic signature needs at least eight
good patterns, at least as many :math:`(4,2,1)` as :math:`(4,2,2)`, at least
eight :math:`(4,2,1)`, and :math:`q \ge 0.97`. Six or seven :math:`(4,2,1)`
patterns are not labeled cubic. With :math:`q \ge 0.97` they are passed
to the fit. Below that, the fit runs only when the conditions in the
next section hold. A partial
hexagonal signature needs at least eight good patterns, more
:math:`(4,2,2)` than :math:`(4,2,1)`, at least four :math:`(4,2,2)`, and the same
:math:`q \ge 0.97`.

A second shell whose radii differ by more than a factor 1.45 does not
receive this signature. It can still be fitted when :math:`q \ge 0.85`, the
longest of the four bonds is at most 1.20 times the shortest, and the
second-shell radii differ by at most a factor 1.6.

An ideal shell, equal bonds and one second-shell radius, is named from
the raw cosine of each second-shell bond with another first-shell bond
and does not build this signature. The bond vectors are not projected.
On the ideal cell that cosine is :math:`1/3` for a staggered arm and :math:`7/9`
for an eclipsed arm. The fast path compares the squares, :math:`1/9` and
:math:`49/81`. An arm is eclipsed when the best square is at least 0.36,
and staggered when that square is at most 0.25. Four staggered arms are
ice Ic. One eclipsed arm and three staggered arms are ice Ih.

The fit
-------

A shell is fitted when the fingerprint did not name it, it already has
one crystal neighbour, :math:`q \ge 0.85`, the longest bond is at most 1.20
times the shortest, and the second-shell radius varies by at most a
factor 1.6. Both clouds are centered. The optimal rotation is a
quaternion (Horn, *Journal of the Optical Society of America A* 4, 629,
1987, `10.1364/JOSAA.4.000629 <https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.4.000629>`_;
Coutsias, Seok and Dill, *Journal of Computational Chemistry* 25, 1849,
2004, `10.1002/jcc.20110 <https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20110>`_). The
residual is the root-mean-square distance after that rotation, divided
by the template radius, the normalization Larsen, Schmidt and Schiøtz
use. Zero is a perfect shape. The ceiling is ``kShellRmsd``, 0.15:



.. math::

    \frac{\mathrm{RMSD}}{R_{\mathrm{template}}} \le 0.15.

``seams symmetry`` uses that ceiling. A positive ``shell_rmsd`` on
``seams_system_symmetry`` or ``seams_symmetry_classify_tol`` replaces it.
A non-positive value selects 0.15 again.

The bond-angle fingerprint names the one template the fit tries. This
cosine is the azimuthal one: both bonds are projected onto the plane
perpendicular to the central bond. Ideal staggered ice lies at 60
degrees, cosine :math:`1/2`, and ideal eclipsed ice lies at 0 degrees,
cosine 1. An arm above 0.90 is eclipsed. Four eclipsed arms are left
unmatched, except on the recovery pass, where that pattern is
hexagonal. The eclipsed arm is anchored to the eclipsed arm of the
template. The four-point orientation uses one Jacobi sweep and the
seventeen-atom residual uses two. A shell that is still far after a few
correspondences is left unmatched, and a shell with only one crystal
neighbour is abandoned sooner than a shell with two.

After the labels are written, a crystal atom that has no crystal
neighbour among its four bonds is returned to unmatched. Hexagonal
recovery is one pass over the atoms left unmatched. It tries an atom
that already has three crystal neighbours, and on that pass four
eclipsed arms can be ice Ih.

Cages, rings, and the other codes
---------------------------------

The same in-tree matcher names a cage or a ring. It is a 3×3 SVD with
the permutation fixed by the bond graph. The continuous shape measure
of that overlay is the scaled residual of Pinsky and Avnir (*Inorganic Chemistry* 37, 5575, 1998,
`10.1021/ic9804925 <https://doi.org/10.1021/ic9804925>`_) and of the
Alvarez shape measure (*Coordination Chemistry Reviews* 249, 1693,
2005, `10.1016/j.ccr.2005.03.031 <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2005.03.031>`_).

IRA (Gunde, Salles, Hémeryck and Martin-Samos, *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling* 61, 5446, 2021,
`10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567 <https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567>`_)
is an optional second matcher for the same templates. SOFI, from the
same group (*Journal of Chemical Physics* 161, 062503, 2024,
`10.1063/5.0215689 <https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0215689>`_), names the
point group. spglib (Togo, Shinohara and Tanaka, *Science and Technology of Advanced Materials: Methods* 4, 2384822, 2024,
`10.1080/27660400.2024.2384822 <https://doi.org/10.1080/27660400.2024.2384822>`_)
names the space group. CHILL+ (Nguyen and Molinero, *Journal of Physical Chemistry B* 119, 9369, 2015,
`10.1021/jp510289t <https://doi.org/10.1021/jp510289t>`_) is a different
label. The two CHILL+ rules are on
`CHILL+: the published rule and the OVITO rule <chill-plus-rules.rst>`_.

A build uses IRA and SOFI when libira is linked, and spglib when
libsymspg is linked. Without libira the matcher is the in-tree Kabsch
search. ``pointGroup`` returns status 2 when SOFI is absent.
``spaceGroup`` returns status 2 when spglib is absent, and
``seams symmetry`` then prints ``space-group off``.

What the command prints
-----------------------

``seams symmetry`` prints the matcher, a space-group line, one shell
overlay when a crystal shell exists, and the tally. The names are
``ice-Ih``, ``ice-Ic``, ``ice-Isd``, and ``unmatched``.

.. code:: bash

    seams symmetry input/traj/ice1h.lammpstrj --type 1
    seams symmetry input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj --type 1

On the 128-molecule ice Ih cell the tally is ``shells ice-Ih 128 unmatched 0``. On the 4096-molecule mW cubic cell it is ``shells ice-Ic 4096 unmatched 0``. A build without libira reports ``symmetry backend kabsch``.

The same call on the shipped frames, with this ceiling:

.. table::

    +-----------------------------+-----------+--------+--------+---------------------+-----------+
    | frame                       | molecules | ice Ih | ice Ic | stacking-disordered | unmatched |
    +=============================+===========+========+========+=====================+===========+
    | ``interface_f0.lammpstrj``  |       864 |    245 |      0 |                   0 |       619 |
    +-----------------------------+-----------+--------+--------+---------------------+-----------+
    | ``nucleation_f1.lammpstrj`` |      4096 |      8 |     12 |                   1 |      4075 |
    +-----------------------------+-----------+--------+--------+---------------------+-----------+

Published CHILL+ on those frames is a wider crystal. The interface slab
is hexagonal 352, cubic 1, interfacial 49, clathrate 3, interfacial
clathrate 25 and water 434. The nucleation frame is hexagonal 62, cubic
47, interfacial 240, clathrate 20, interfacial clathrate 138 and water

1. CHILL+ cubic and hexagonal need four bond correlations in the

staggered or eclipsed windows. The shell also asks for the
common-neighbour signature or a residual at most 0.15, and for a
crystal neighbour. CHILL+ has an interfacial label. The shell does not,
so two or three staggered bonds stay unmatched.

C++, in ``symm``, ``classifyIce`` writes one label per row. The default
ceiling is ``kShellRmsd`` (0.15), the tetrahedral cutoff is ``kThermalQ``
(0.85), and the bond-length ratio is ``kThermalBondRatio`` (1.20).
``classifyIceArrays`` is the same call on three coordinate columns.
``best``, ``matchKernel`` and ``matchAll`` name one environment. The template
names are ``ice-Ih``, ``ice-Ic``, ``ice-Isd``, ``5^12``, ``5^12 6^2``,
``5^12 6^4``, ``ring-5`` and ``ring-6``.

C, in ``seams_c_api.h``, ``seams_symmetry_classify`` writes the same labels
at the library ceiling. ``seams_symmetry_classify_tol`` takes an explicit
ceiling. ``seams_symmetry_classify_gates`` also takes the tetrahedral
cutoff and the bond-length ratio.

Python is ``dseams.symmetry`` in
`PydSEAMSlib <https://github.com/d-SEAMS/PydSEAMSlib>`_.
The per-atom integers are the ones above.
