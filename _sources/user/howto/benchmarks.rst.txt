==========
Benchmarks
==========


Time a 2.14.0 build and write down the run. The guest-code medians are
on `Literature head-to-head <literature-h2h.rst>`_. The thread
width each guest receives is on
`Reproduce the literature head-to-head <literature-reproduce.rst>`_.
The pinned neighbour session is
`Cutoff neighbour lists <neighbour-lists.rst>`_.

Build
-----

``pixi run build`` configures ``bbdir`` with ``-Dwith_tests=true`` and
compiles it. The same two steps by hand:

.. code:: bash

    meson setup bbdir --prefix=$CONDA_PREFIX -Dwith_tests=true
    ninja -C bbdir

The timers are ``bbdir/tests/bench_*``. ``pixi run test`` runs the test
suite and does not run them.

A host-only build takes the host branch of the device code.
``SEAMS_OFFLOAD=0`` forces that path. ``SEAMS_OFFLOAD=1`` selects the
offload path when the build has one. Record the host, the compiler,
the commit, the thread count, the cpu affinity, and ``SEAMS_OFFLOAD``.

The comparisons below use the Python scripts under ``scripts/``.

Neighbour list
--------------

``bench_cutoff_list`` times ``nneigh::orthoFullCsr`` and ``neighListO`` on
ideal ice Ic. The lattice constant is 6.35 Å and the cutoff is 3.5 Å.
Molecules must be ``8 n^3``. The arguments are molecules, threads, and
repetitions. The defaults are 110592, 1, and 8. Every repetition is a
sample. The printed median is the sample at index ``n/2`` after sorting.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_cutoff_list 110592 1 8

``scripts/bench_neighbours_threads.py`` runs that binary and the same
list in vesin, freud, and matscipy, at 10648, 110592, and 884736
molecules and at 1, 2, 4, and 8 threads. Set ``NEIGH_BENCH`` to the
binary. The script's own default is
``build-release/tests/bench_cutoff_list``. ``--reps`` defaults to 8.
``--out`` writes the lines.

Triclinic list, CHILL+, rings, and diamond
------------------------------------------

``bench_v2`` takes a mode, a molecule count, a thread count, and a
repetition count. Molecules must be ``8 n^3``. ``tilt`` builds the
triclinic cell, with ``xy = 0.25 L``, ``xz = 0.05 L``, and ``yz = 0.12 L``.
Any other mode uses the orthorhombic cell. The cutoff is 3.5 Å and
the neighbour build is inside the timer. The first call is the
warm-up and is dropped. The printed median is again the sample at
index ``n/2`` after sorting.

.. table::

    +--------------+----------------------------------------------------+
    | mode         | what the timer includes                            |
    +==============+====================================================+
    | ``tilt``     | the triclinic cutoff list                          |
    +--------------+----------------------------------------------------+
    | ``chill``    | that list, then CHILL+                             |
    +--------------+----------------------------------------------------+
    | ``rings``    | that list, then primitive rings to depth 6         |
    +--------------+----------------------------------------------------+
    | ``symmetry`` | that list, then the diamond common-neighbour label |
    +--------------+----------------------------------------------------+

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_v2 tilt 110592 1 8

``rings`` is a full search of one frame. The carried update is
``bench_hop_stride``, below. ``scripts/bench_neighbours_v2.py`` drives
``bench_v2`` and the matching vesin, freud, mdapy, and OVITO calls.
Set ``NEIGH_BENCH``. The script's default is
``bbdir-release/tests/bench_v2``. An output path, when given, is the
first argument. ``NEIGH_ONLY`` limits the matrix to
``mode:molecules:threads``.

Local q4 and q6
---------------

``bench_ql`` reads a frame: a little-endian ``uint32`` count, a ``float64``
box length, then ``n`` times three ``float64`` coordinates. The arguments
are the frame, the degree, and the number of timed calls. One warm-up
runs first. The neighbour build, at cutoff 3.5 Å, is inside every
sample. A last path writes the warm-up :math:`q_l` as raw ``float64``.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_ql frame.bin 6 8

``scripts/bench_ql_threads.py`` builds ideal ice Ic at bond 2.75 Å and
one shifted liquid, and times degrees 4 and 6 against OVITO and
freud. ``--out`` is required. The binary is ``SEAMS_QL_BENCH``, or
``bbdir-release/tests/bench_ql``, or ``bbdir/tests/bench_ql``. One
warm-up, then eight samples. The median of an even count is the mean
of the two central values, and the interquartile range is
Hyndman-Fan type 7.

Primitive rings against OVITO
-----------------------------

``bench_rings_h2h`` times the ring search on a cutoff graph built
before the timer. ``--repl`` reads commands: ``go`` repeats that search,
``in`` times the neighbour list, the index, and the search together,
and ``quit`` stops.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_rings_h2h input/traj/exampleTraj.lammpstrj 1 2 3.5 6 8 2

The arguments after the dump are frame, atom type, cutoff, maximum
ring, repetitions, and warm-up. The defaults for the last two are 8
and 2. Type 2 is the oxygen in ``exampleTraj.lammpstrj``.

``scripts/rings_one_thread.py`` runs the binary and OVITO
``CreateBondsModifier`` plus ``FindRingsModifier`` at one thread and at
eight. ``--bench`` is the binary and ``--json`` is the output path.

Cage stage
----------

Run ``bench_cages`` from ``input/``. The default dump is
``traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj``, frame 1, three repetitions, atom type 1.
The later arguments are the HC template, the DDC template, and the
atom type. The templates in the tree are ``templates/hc.xyz`` and
``templates/ddc.xyz``.

.. code:: bash

    cd input && ../bbdir/tests/bench_cages

Each printed time is the fastest repetition. The lines are the ring
network to depth 7, ``findHC``, ``findDDC``, both of those, local :math:`q_6`,
and, when the templates load, the shape match. A nauty certificate of
the six-rings is included when nauty is linked.

``bench_ddc_ladder`` times ``findDDC`` alone. Neighbours, rings, and
``findHC`` run before the timer. The arguments are the dump, the
warm-up count, and the repetition count.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_ddc_ladder input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj 1 8

Incremental updates
-------------------

``bench_churn_sweep`` moves a ball of molecules from frame A to frame B
and times ``RingUpdater`` and ``AffiliationUpdater`` against a full pass.
Each printed time is the median of the repetitions. Every incremental
result is checked against the batch result.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_churn_sweep input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj

The optional arguments are frame A, frame B, cutoff, atom type,
repetitions, a ring full-pass fraction, and an affiliation batch
fraction.

``bench_trajectory_incremental`` walks a dump. ``RingUpdater`` is checked
against ``ringNetwork``, and ``AffiliationUpdater`` against the batch
affiliation. The graph is ``cutoff``, ``knn``, or ``knn-union``. The
default graph is ``knn``.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_trajectory_incremental input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj

The optional arguments are the frame count, the atom type, the
maximum ring depth, the skin, and the graph.

``bench_hop_stride`` times the carried primitive-ring update against a
full search of the same frame. The graphs are the 3.5 Å cutoff list
and the seeded pair used by ``seams cages``: mutual and union
4-nearest, candidate cutoff 5 Å, rings through depth 6. The names in
the log are ``cutoff``, ``seeded-strict``, ``seeded-union``, and ``seeded``.
One warm-up, then eight repetitions, unless ``--reps`` and ``--warmup``
say otherwise. The printed carry and rebuild times are type-7
medians. The printed speedup is the sum of the per-frame median
rebuild times divided by the sum of the per-frame median carry times.
A transition is inside the ball when the update enumerates fewer
sources than the molecule count.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_hop_stride time input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj --graph both

``crossover`` uses ``--frame-a`` and ``--frame-b``. ``--type`` selects the
atom type. The carried-ring definition is on
`Linkcell neighbours and carried updates <linkcell-carried.rst>`_.

Device batch
------------

``bench_gpu_batch`` reads a dump and prints ``resident yes`` or
``resident no``. A host build that cannot keep the frames on the device
stops there. When they stay resident, the log has the cold
upload, compute, and download, then the warm repeat with the smallest
compute time.

.. code:: bash

    bbdir/tests/bench_gpu_batch input/traj/mW_cubic.lammpstrj

The optional arguments are the frame count, the atom type, and the
number of warm repeats. The defaults are 11, 1, and 5.
