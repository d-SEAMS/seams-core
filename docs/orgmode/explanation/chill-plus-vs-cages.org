#+TITLE: CHILL+ versus cages
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

Two classifiers live in ~libyodaLib~.
They answer different questions and they do not imply each other.

* Four-neighbour star

CHILL (~seams chill~, ~chill::getCorrel~ / ~getIceTypeNoPrint~) and CHILL+ (~seams chill-plus~, ~chill::getCorrelPlus~ / ~getIceTypePlusNoPrint~) look at the four-neighbour star.
Both stay on the host.
A crystal label needs exactly four neighbours inside the cutoff. That shell count is taken before the bond list is cut to four. The old count after the cut was a bug: a shell of five or more neighbours could still receive an ice label. q3 is then the average over the four nearest neighbours of a four-neighbour shell. The staggered and eclipsed pattern of that star labels the atom cubic, hexagonal, interfacial, clathrate, or water.

A molecule is cubic or hexagonal when its four bonds look like ice, even if those neighbours do not close a cage.

CHILL+: Nguyen and Molinero, *J. Phys. Chem. B* 2015 ([[https://doi.org/10.1021/jp510289t][10.1021/jp510289t]]).
CHILL: Moore et al., *Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys.* 2010.

* Cage membership

The ice score (~seams cages~, ~ring::seededCageAffiliation~ by default) is cage membership.
A molecule in a hexagonal cage (HC) is ice Ih.
A molecule in a double-diamond cage (DDC) is ice Ic.
A molecule in neither is water.
The cages are assembled from six-membered primitive rings on a chosen bond graph.

This is topological unit matching (TUM).
The methods paper is the 2020 JCIM article (see [[file:citation.org][Citation]]).

The GPU path (~gpu::analyzeResident~) is that TUM ice score only: ~linkcell::gpu~ k-nearest, the mutual four-nearest graph, primitive six-rings, and HC/DDC affiliation.
CHILL, CHILL+, and \(q_{lm}\) stay on the host.
Only cage labels come back.

* Why they disagree

A configuration can agree exactly on the closed star of a vertex (same induced graph, same coordinates on those five atoms) and still disagree on that vertex's cage label, because the cage predicates read a larger neighbourhood of six-rings.
The Catch2 suite encodes that fact: cage membership is not a function of the four-neighbour star.

On the in-tree mixed TIP4P dump (~input/traj/exampleTraj.lammpstrj~), ~seams chill-plus~ uses the cutoff list by default and prints 9 interfacial clathrate and 241 water; ~--graph mutual~ prints 1 clathrate, 7 interfacial clathrate, and 242 water.
Seeded cages report zero HC and zero DDC.
A three-eclipsed star is not cage membership.
The commands are in [[file:../tutorials/bulk-ice.org][the mixed TIP4P tutorial]].

* Bond graph

~seams cages --graph~ chooses the bond graph those rings are found on:

| value | graph |
|-------+-------|
| ~cutoff~ | pairs inside the distance cutoff |
| ~knn~ | mutual k-nearest (default ~k=4~) |
| ~knn-union~ | union k-nearest (completion graph of the seeded rule) |
| ~seeded~ | mutual seeds, union completions (CLI default) |

On a perfect cubic crystal the four graphs coincide.
On a mixed nucleation dump they do not have to.

Pool-audit ~ac510645~ records the fix in ~18588dac~. The seeded cage call searches once when the mutual and union edge sets match. ~fellBack()~ runs before a dirty frame returns, so the following frames are warm sweeps.

* Ring-adjacent completion

~ring::seededCageAffiliation~ takes a fifth argument, ~ringAdjacentCompletion~, default ~false~.
When the flag is ~true~, the accepted labels pass through ~ring::ringAdjacentCompletion~: a permissive six-ring with all but one vertex labelled fills that last vertex, iterated to a fixed point.
Hexagonal cage (HC) flags and double-diamond cage (DDC) flags run as two walks.

~seams cages --complete~ sets the flag.
The rule, the least-fixed-point claim, and the Lean build live on [[file:ring-completion.org][Ring-adjacent completion]].
