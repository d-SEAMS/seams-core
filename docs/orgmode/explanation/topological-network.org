#+TITLE: Topological network criteria
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

This page is the definitions.
The runnable checks that the implementations still return the documented counts live under Tutorials.

* Bond correlation

CHILL and CHILL+ look at \(l = 3\).
With \(N_b(i)\) neighbours,

\[
q_{3m}(i) = \frac{1}{N_b(i)} \sum_{j \in N(i)} Y_{3m}(\hat r_{ij})
\]

\[
c_{ij} = \mathrm{Re}
\frac{\sum_m q_{3m}(i)\, q_{3m}(j)^{*}}
{\sqrt{\sum_m |q_{3m}(i)|^2 \sum_m |q_{3m}(j)|^2}}
\]

A bond is staggered when \(c_{ij} \le -0.8\).
The eclipsed window is the part that differs:

| rule | eclipsed | staggered | neighbours |
|------+----------+-----------+------------|
| CHILL | \(-0.2 \le c_{ij} \le -0.05\) | \(c_{ij} \le -0.8\) | 4 |
| CHILL+ | \(-0.35 \le c_{ij} \le 0.25\) | \(c_{ij} \le -0.8\) | 4 |

The code stores those edges as =chill::chillRule= =(-0.8, -0.2, -0.05, 4)= and =chill::chillPlusRule= =(-0.8, -0.35, 0.25, 4)=.
Boundaries are inclusive.

The four-bond star is the phase.
Cubic is four staggered bonds.
Hexagonal is three staggered and one eclipsed.
CHILL+ adds clathrate (four eclipsed) and interfacial clathrate (three eclipsed).
Water is what remains.
The full star table is in =bop.hpp=.

The default =chill+= rule follows Nguyen and Molinero.
Table 1 footnote a says "Number of water neighbors within 3.5 Å (first coordination shell)."
A crystal row requires that count to be 4, so a fifth oxygen inside the cutoff is water even when four nearer bonds would have been ice-like.
Equation 1 is still the average over the four nearest neighbours,

\[
q_{lm}(i) = \frac{1}{4} \sum_{j=1}^{4} Y_{lm}(\hat r_{ij}).
\]

The interfacial rows keep the neighbour test in the comment column of Table 1.
=ovito-chill+= (=--rule ovito-chill+=, or the =ovito-chill+= command) still requires exactly four neighbours inside the cutoff, averages =q_3= over every neighbour inside the cutoff, and does not apply the neighbour test.
That is the OVITO 3.17.2 Chill+ modifier.
Plain CHILL is still the four closest neighbours and Moore's neighbour test, and it does not require the cutoff shell to hold exactly four neighbours.

* Primitive rings

A cycle is primitive, in the Franzblau sense, when for every pair of its vertices a shortest path in the bond graph lies along the cycle.
=primitive::ringNetwork= enumerates cycles up to =max_depth= and drops the ones that fail that test.
The bond graph is an argument.
Mutual four-nearest, the union of those nominations, and a distance cutoff are three different graphs.
Quote the graph with the census.

* Cages

A hexagonal cage is two basal six-rings joined by prismatic six-rings.
A double-diamond cage is an equatorial six-ring and its peripheral rings.
=ring::cageAffiliation= evaluates those conditions for every six-ring, without the claim order of the older =findHC= / =findDDC= walk.
The seeded variant takes a strict graph for the seeds and a permissive graph for the completions.
An atom on the permissive graph is kept only when its component contains a seed.
=--complete= then fills a six-ring that is missing one labelled vertex.
That walk is the least fixed point of the all-but-one rule; see the ring-completion page.

* Confined ice

A prism block is two rings of the same size that satisfy the basal conditions in =ring::basalPrismConditions= (or the relaxed form).
=ring::findPrisms= returns which rings are blocks, split into perfect and imperfect.
A monolayer analysis keeps primitive rings of each size and, when you ask for the files, writes the coverage of the sheet.
The in-plane \(g(r)\) normalises the pair histogram by the slab factor

\[
f(r) =
\begin{cases}
1 - r / (2h) & r \le h \\
h / (2r) & r > h
\end{cases}
\]

where \(h\) is the confined height.
That is =rdf2::normalizeRDF=.

* Order parameters

\[
q_l(i) = \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{2l+1} \sum_{m=-l}^{l} |q_{lm}(i)|^2}
\]

\(\bar q_l(i)\) replaces \(q_{lm}(i)\) with the average of \(q_{lm}\) over \(i\) and its neighbours before the same sum.
That is the Lechner-Dellago form.
The implementation is =chill::steinhardtQl=.
