#+TITLE: Smooth collective variables
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

A biased simulation (metadynamics, OPES) needs a collective variable
that is a differentiable function of the coordinates, with its gradient.
A Markov state model built on soft memberships needs the same. The ring
census of [[file:primitive-rings.org][primitive rings]] and the cage
search are integer counts: they jump when a bond crosses the cutoff, and
their gradient is zero almost everywhere.

The ~seams_cv~ handle of =include/seams.h= gives smooth versions of those
counts, each with its gradient in the Cartesian coordinates of every
atom, under periodic boundaries. With bond weights of exactly zero and
one they reduce to the integer counts. This page gives the formulas,
the argument that they are continuous, the C API, a worked example, and
what they do not cover.

* The switching function

Every pair \(i, j\) at minimum-image distance \(r\) gets a weight

\[
s(r) = R(r)\,T(r), \qquad
R(r) = \frac{1 - x^n}{1 - x^m}, \quad x = \frac{r - d_0}{r_0},
\]

with \(R = 1\) for \(r \le d_0\). This is the PLUMED ~RATIONAL~ switch.
The library evaluates it as the ratio of the geometric sums
\(1 + x + \dots + x^{n-1}\) and \(1 + x + \dots + x^{m-1}\), so
\(x = 1\) needs no special case; there \(R = n/m\) and
\(R' = -n(m-n) / (2 m r_0)\). Above \(x = 1\) it uses
\(R = y^{m-n} G_n(y) / G_m(y)\) with \(y = 1/x\) and \(G_k\) the
geometric sum, which forms no power of \(x\): \(s\) is finite for any
exponents and any finite distance. Only \(ds/dr\) can leave double
range, when \(r_0\) or \(d_{max} - d_{on}\) is near the smallest
doubles, and the API then returns ~SEAMS_ERR_RANGE~ rather than a
non-finite number. ~m = 0~ selects \(m = 2n\), as in PLUMED.

PLUMED cuts \(R\) at ~D_MAX~ with a step, which leaves a jump of
\(R(d_{max})\) in the value. Here the cut is a taper,

\[
T(r) = 1 - u^3 (10 - 15u + 6u^2), \qquad u = \frac{r - d_{on}}{d_{max} - d_{on}},
\]

with \(T = 1\) below \(d_{on}\) and \(T = 0\) from \(d_{max}\) on. \(T\)
and its first two derivatives are continuous at both ends, so \(s\) is
\(C^2\) at \(d_{on}\) and at \(d_{max}\), and exactly zero beyond
\(d_{max}\). Near \(d_0\), \(R = 1 - x^n + O(x^m)\). At \(d_0 > 0\)
\(s\) is therefore \(C^{n-1}\) there and not \(C^n\). At \(d_0 = 0\)
the same expansion in \(r = |v|\), with \(v\) the bond vector, is
\(C^2\) in \(v\) for \(n \ge 2\), but for \(n = 1\) it is the cone
\(1 - |v|/r_0\), which has no derivative where the two atoms coincide.
The bond weights are \(C^2\) in the coordinates for \(n \ge 3\), and
for \(n = 2\) with \(d_0 = 0\). At \(r = d_0\) the library reports the
right derivative, \(-1/r_0\) for \(n = 1\) and 0 for \(n \ge 2\). The
parameters must satisfy \(r_0 > 0\), \(1 \le n < m\) and
\(0 \le d_0 \le d_{on} < d_{max}\).

~seams_switch_rational()~ returns \(s\) and \(ds/dr\) for one distance.

* Soft primitive rings

A ring is primitive when no two of its members are closer through the
graph than around the ring (Franzblau). Weighting only the primitive
rings of the graph inside \(d_{max}\) is discontinuous: when a chord of
a square leaves the graph at \(d_{max}\), with weight zero, the square
becomes primitive and its weight \(s_1 s_2 s_3 s_4\) appears at once.

The soft weight therefore carries the primitivity test as well. The
candidate rings are every simple cycle of at most ~max_ring~ members on
the listed graph. For a candidate ring \(C\),

\[
w_C = \prod_{e \in C} s_e \prod_{P \in \mathcal{S}(C)} \Bigl(1 - \prod_{e \in P} s_e\Bigr),
\]

where \(\mathcal{S}(C)\) is the set of shortcut paths of \(C\): simple
paths between two members \(a, b\) of \(C\) with fewer edges than the
separation of \(a\) and \(b\) around \(C\). A path may pass other
members of \(C\). With weights in \(\{0, 1\}\), \(w_C = 1\) exactly when
\(C\) is a cycle of the graph with no shortcut, which is Franzblau's
test, and \(w_C = 0\) otherwise. =test_smooth_cv= checks that the rings of
weight one on ice1h at 3.5 A are the rings ~seams_rings_find~ returns.

The variables built on the ring weights are

- ~SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT~, \(\sum_C w_C\) over the rings of size ~arg~
  (0 for every size);
- ~SEAMS_CV_RING_ATOMS~, \(\sum_i p_i\) over the selected atoms, with
  \(p_i = 1 - \prod_{C \ni i} (1 - w_C)\), the soft count of atoms on a
  ring of size ~arg~;
- ~SEAMS_CV_HEX_BASAL_PAIRS~, \(\sum_{(A,B)} w_A w_B\,\beta_{AB}\) over
  vertex-disjoint candidate hexagons, where \(\beta_{AB}\) is
  ~ring::basalConditions~ in either direction with every "and" a product
  and every "some" a \(1 - \prod (1 - \cdot)\) over the bond weights
  between the two rings.

With 0/1 weights the last one is the number of hexagon pairs that pass
the basal test of the hexagonal-cage search, checked against
~ring::basalConditions~ on ice1h. It is the smooth form of that test
alone. ~ring::findHC~ goes on to take prismatic rings and to claim rings
in visiting order, and the double-diamond search shares rings between
cages; those steps are choices, so the cage labels, the DDC count and the
per-ring symmetry codes have no smooth counterpart here.

* Continuity

Each of the three ring variables above is a fixed polynomial in the
weights \(s_{ij}\) of the pairs of the complete graph on the selected
atoms, of bounded degree
for a given ~max_ring~. The neighbour list only decides which terms are
written down. A term left out holds an unlisted pair, whose weight is
zero, as a factor, so it is zero; a shortcut factor left out is
\(1 - 0 = 1\). The value computed from a list is therefore the value of
the full polynomial, for any list that holds every pair inside
\(d_{max}\).

~seams_cv_rebuild()~ lists the pairs within \(d_{max} + \text{skin}\).
While no selected atom has moved more than \(\text{skin}/2\) since the
rebuild, no pair distance has shrunk by more than the skin, so every
pair now inside \(d_{max}\) is listed. The candidate rings, shortcut
paths and hexagon pairs are fixed at the rebuild and cover every term
with a nonzero weight on that list. Between rebuilds the variable is the
same polynomial, so it is as smooth as \(s\); across a rebuild the value
does not change, because both lists give the full polynomial. The
minimum-image distance is smooth while \(d_{max} + \text{skin}\) is
below half the narrowest cell width, which ~seams_cv_rebuild()~ requires
(~SEAMS_ERR_BOX~ otherwise).

~seams_cv_needs_rebuild()~ reports when an atom has moved more than half
the skin, the atom count changed or the cell changed. ~seams_cv_value()~
and ~seams_cv_states()~ then return ~SEAMS_ERR_STATE~ until the next
rebuild, so a stale topology is never evaluated.

=test_smooth_cv= checks this on a rhombus whose short diagonal sweeps
through \(d_{max}\): the count from a fresh rebuild at every step equals
the count from one topology that holds the diagonal, the value and its
slope change by no more than the switch bounds allow per step, and at
\(x = 1\) the square keeps exactly half its weight. A second case
compares a list at \(d_{max}\) with one that adds the near edge of the
next shell of ice: the extra rings, paths and pairs change no value or
gradient beyond rounding.

* Hull surrogate

There is no convex hull in the engine, and the hull volume is only
piecewise smooth: its gradient jumps when a facet changes. The cluster
size variables use the weighted gyration tensor instead. With weights
\(q_i\) on the selected atoms, \(W = \sum q_i\), \(c = \sum q_i u_i / W\),

\[
G = \frac{1}{W} \sum_i q_i (u_i - c)(u_i - c)^\top, \qquad
R_g^2 = \operatorname{tr} G, \qquad
V = \tfrac{4\pi}{3}\, 5^{3/2} \sqrt{\det G}.
\]

\(V\) is the volume of the uniform solid ellipsoid with gyration tensor
\(G\). ~arg = -1~ gives every selected atom \(q_i = 1\); ~arg = 0~ or a
ring size gives \(q_i = p_i\), the soft ring participation above, so the
size follows the atoms that sit on rings. The gradients are

\[
\frac{\partial R_g^2}{\partial u_i} = \frac{2 q_i (u_i - c)}{W}, \qquad
\frac{\partial R_g^2}{\partial q_i} = \frac{|u_i - c|^2 - R_g^2}{W},
\]
\[
\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_i} = \frac{V q_i}{W}\, G^{-1}(u_i - c), \qquad
\frac{\partial V}{\partial q_i} = \frac{V}{2W}\Bigl((u_i - c)^\top G^{-1} (u_i - c) - 3\Bigr),
\]

and \(\partial q_i / \partial x\) runs back through the ring weights to
the bond weights. The positions \(u_i\) are unwrapped along the listed
bonds from the first selected atom, so a cluster that straddles the
cell boundary is measured whole.

These two are not polynomials in the pair weights, and they are smooth
only where three conditions hold. The selection must be compact: in
some periodic image its atoms are all closer to each other than half the
narrowest cell width. Every bond step and every placement then lands on
that image, whatever the graph, so the \(u_i\) are smooth functions of
the coordinates and a fresh rebuild gives the same value. The total weight \(W\) must be positive: \(R_g^2\) and \(V\) do
not depend on the scale of the \(q_i\), so they do not tend to 0 as
\(W\) does, and at \(W = 0\) they are 0. For \(V\), \(\det G\) must be
positive: where the cluster becomes flat \(V\) is continuous but in
general not differentiable. Where these hold, \(R_g^2\) and \(V\) are as
smooth as the weights \(q_i\), which with ~arg = -1~ is \(C^\infty\).

* Soft states

A state is a product of clauses. A clause on a variable \(v\) is
\(\sigma((v - t)/w)\) when ~above~ is nonzero and
\(1 - \sigma((v - t)/w)\) otherwise, with \(\sigma(z) = 1/(1 + e^{-z})\).
A clause of kind ~SEAMS_CV_STATE~ with width 0 is \(\chi_k\) or
\(1 - \chi_k\) of an earlier state \(k\). Products are "and", the
complement is "not", and "or" is \(1 - (1 - \chi_a)(1 - \chi_b)\): a state
of two complement clauses, then the complement of that state. Each
\(\chi\) lies in \([0, 1]\), and its gradient is the product rule over
the clauses with the cached gradient of each variable.

* Gradients

All gradients are reverse mode. The adjoint of the ring weights is
formed first, then pushed onto the bond weights with leave-one-out
products, so a bond weight of zero inside a product is handled without
dividing by it. The soft basal term uses forward-mode dual numbers over
its 36 bond weights and two ring weights. A bond weight reaches the
coordinates through \(\partial s / \partial x_j = s'(r)\,\hat{r}_{ij}\)
and \(\partial s / \partial x_i = -s'(r)\,\hat{r}_{ij}\), with
\(\hat{r}_{ij}\) the minimum-image unit vector from \(i\) to \(j\).

=test_smooth_cv= compares every gradient with central differences at
\(h = 10^{-5}\) A on jittered ice and on clusters that straddle the cell
boundary, to \(10^{-6}(1 + |g|)\).

* C API

| Call | Role |
|------+------|
| ~seams_switch_rational(r, r0, d0, n, m, d_on, d_max, &s, &dsdr)~ | \(s(r)\) and \(ds/dr\) |
| ~seams_cv_create(r0, d0, n, m, d_on, d_max, max_ring, &cv)~ | switch and largest ring, 3 to 10 |
| ~seams_cv_rebuild(cv, sys, type, atoms, n_atoms, skin)~ | list, candidate rings, paths and pairs |
| ~seams_cv_needs_rebuild(cv, sys, &stale)~ | 1 after a move beyond skin / 2 or a new cell |
| ~seams_cv_topology(cv, &n_edges, &n_rings, &n_pairs)~ | sizes of the fixed topology |
| ~seams_cv_value(cv, sys, kind, arg, &value, grad, grad_len)~ | one variable and \(3N\) gradient entries |
| ~seams_cv_state_clause(cv, state, kind, arg, threshold, width, above)~ | add a clause; ~state~ equal to the count opens a new state |
| ~seams_cv_states(cv, sys, chi, chi_len, &n_states, grad, grad_len)~ | every \(\chi\), and \(n_{states} \times 3N\) gradient entries |
| ~seams_cv_destroy(cv)~ | free the handle |

~kind~ is a ~seams_cv_kind~: ~SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT~,
~SEAMS_CV_RING_ATOMS~, ~SEAMS_CV_HEX_BASAL_PAIRS~,
~SEAMS_CV_GYRATION_RG2~, ~SEAMS_CV_GYRATION_VOLUME~ or ~SEAMS_CV_STATE~.
The gradient is in units of the variable per angstrom, laid out as
~grad[3 * i + d]~ for atom ~i~ and axis ~d~, and zero for atoms outside
the selection. The signatures and status codes are on :doc:`/stable/c`.

* Worked example

One planar hexagon of side 2.75 A sits on the \(x = 0\) face of a
20 A cell, so two of its bonds are measured through the boundary. With
\(r_0 = 0.3\), \(d_0 = 2.6\), \(n = 6\), \(m = 12\), \(x = 0.5\) and
\(s = 64/65\). The ring weight is \(s^6\), and growing the hexagon by
\(dR\) lengthens every bond by \(dR\), so
\(dw/dR = 6 s^5\, ds/dr\). The program checks both against the API and
exits nonzero when they differ.

#+begin_src c
#include "seams.h"

#include <math.h>
#include <stdio.h>

int main(void) {
  const double pi = acos(-1.0);
  const double side = 2.75;
  double xyz[18];
  for (int k = 0; k < 6; ++k) {
    const double t = k * pi / 3.0;
    xyz[3 * k] = fmod(20.0 + side * cos(t), 20.0);
    xyz[3 * k + 1] = 10.0 + side * sin(t);
    xyz[3 * k + 2] = 10.0;
  }
  const double box9[9] = {0, 0, 0, 20, 20, 20, 0, 0, 0};
  seams_system *sys = seams_system_create();
  seams_cv *cv = NULL;
  if (sys == NULL || seams_system_set_atoms(sys, xyz, 6, box9, 9, NULL, NULL) != SEAMS_OK ||
      seams_cv_create(0.3, 2.6, 6, 0, 3.0, 3.5, 6, &cv) != SEAMS_OK ||
      seams_cv_rebuild(cv, sys, 0, NULL, 0, 0.5) != SEAMS_OK) {
    fprintf(stderr, "setup: %s\n", seams_last_error());
    return 1;
  }

  double s = 0, dsdr = 0;
  seams_switch_rational(side, 0.3, 2.6, 6, 0, 3.0, 3.5, &s, &dsdr);
  double w = 0, grad[18];
  seams_cv_value(cv, sys, SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT, 6, &w, grad, 18);
  double radial = 0;
  for (int k = 0; k < 6; ++k) {
    const double t = k * pi / 3.0;
    radial += grad[3 * k] * cos(t) + grad[3 * k + 1] * sin(t);
  }
  printf("ring weight %.6f, s^6 %.6f\n", w, pow(s, 6));
  printf("dw/dR %.6f, 6 s^5 ds/dr %.6f\n", radial, 6 * pow(s, 5) * dsdr);
  int bad = fabs(w - pow(s, 6)) > 1e-12 || fabs(radial - 6 * pow(s, 5) * dsdr) > 1e-9;

  /* State 0: a six-ring is present. State 1: it is not. */
  seams_cv_state_clause(cv, 0, SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT, 6, 0.5, 0.1, 1);
  seams_cv_state_clause(cv, 1, SEAMS_CV_STATE, 0, 0.0, 0.0, 0);
  double chi[2], chi_grad[2 * 18];
  size_t n_states = 0;
  seams_cv_states(cv, sys, chi, 2, &n_states, chi_grad, 2 * 18);
  printf("chi %.6f %.6f\n", chi[0], chi[1]);
  bad |= n_states != 2 || fabs(chi[0] + chi[1] - 1.0) > 1e-12;

  /* A move beyond half the skin needs a rebuild before the next value. */
  xyz[1] += 0.3;
  int stale = 0;
  seams_system_set_positions(sys, xyz, 6, NULL, 0);
  seams_cv_needs_rebuild(cv, sys, &stale);
  bad |= stale != 1 || seams_cv_value(cv, sys, SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT, 6, &w, NULL, 0) != SEAMS_ERR_STATE;
  seams_cv_rebuild(cv, sys, 0, NULL, 0, 0.5);
  seams_cv_value(cv, sys, SEAMS_CV_RING_COUNT, 6, &w, NULL, 0);
  printf("stale %d, ring weight after the move %.6f\n", stale, w);

  seams_cv_destroy(cv);
  seams_system_destroy(sys);
  return bad;
}
#+end_src

* Limitations

- The candidate rings are every simple cycle up to ~max_ring~ on the
  list at \(d_{max} + \text{skin}\). Their number grows quickly with the
  coordination of that graph, so a wide skin or a dense liquid with a
  large ~max_ring~ makes the rebuild slow and the topology large. Keep
  \(d_{max} + \text{skin}\) inside the first minimum of the oxygen RDF.
- Only the basal test of the hexagonal cage is smooth. Cage labels,
  double-diamond cages, prism stacks and the per-ring symmetry codes
  stay integer.
- The gyration variables assume a compact selection. Otherwise a part
  of the selection with no listed bond to the rest is placed by its
  minimum image from the first selected atom, which jumps by a cell
  vector where two images are equally near. On a crystal that
  percolates through the cell the unwrapped positions depend on the
  search order and the variables have no meaning.
- With ring weights, \(R_g^2\) and \(V\) jump to 0 when the total ring
  participation reaches 0, for example when the last ring of the size
  breaks. A bias on them should keep a ring present, or use
  ~arg = -1~.
- \(V\) is \(\sqrt{\det G}\): continuous, but in general not
  differentiable where the cluster becomes flat. Lifting one atom a
  height \(h\) off the plane of the others gives \(V \propto |h|\), a
  kink. A flat or empty cluster returns 0 with a zero gradient.
- \(s\) is \(C^{n-1}\) at \(d_0 > 0\). With \(n < 3\) and \(d_0 > 0\) a
  second derivative jumps there. With \(d_0 = 0\) and \(n = 1\) a bond
  weight has a cone where its two atoms coincide, and the gradient
  reported there is zero.
- The rebuild runs on one thread, and a value costs one pass over the
  rings, their shortcut paths and the hexagon pairs. The values are for
  one frame of one handle at a time; concurrent calls need one handle
  each.
- The PLUMED, LAMMPS and OpenMM drivers in =engines/= do not call these
  variables. A bias needs a driver that calls ~seams_cv_value()~ each
  step and adds \(-\partial U / \partial v\) times ~grad~ to the forces.
