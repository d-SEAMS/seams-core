#+TITLE: Local symmetry
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

The engine names a local environment as ice Ih, ice Ic, stacking-disordered ice, a hydrate cage, or a five- or six-ring, and it labels every oxygen in a cell.
The per-atom ice label is the production path.
Cage and ring names, the point group, and the space group are the same templates asked one cluster at a time.

* What is labelled

A crystal oxygen is ice Ih (0), ice Ic (1), or stacking-disordered ice (2).
Anything else is -1.
Only those two diamond structures count as crystal when the same frame is scored with OVITO.
The first-neighbour and second-neighbour tags from identify diamond are not crystal, and polyhedral template matching counts only cubic diamond and hexagonal diamond.

The seventeen-atom shell is the central oxygen, its four nearest neighbours, and the twelve neighbours of those four.
That is the shell polyhedral template matching uses for diamond (Larsen, Schmidt and Schiøtz, /Modelling and Simulation in Materials Science and Engineering/ 24, 055007, 2016, [[https://doi.org/10.1088/0965-0393/24/5/055007][10.1088/0965-0393/24/5/055007]]).

* Neighbours

The cell is orthorhombic.
Atoms are wrapped into the box, histogrammed into cells of the bond cutoff, and stored as three coordinate columns.
The bond cutoff is 3.5 Å and a neighbour closer than 2.15 Å is dropped.
One thread walks each pair once and writes both ends of the bond.
Interior cells use a fixed half of the 27-cell stencil, so that walk does not build a neighbour list for the cell.
The four nearest bonds are kept by distance and then sorted, because the common-neighbour test and the fit both read them in that order.
Eight threads scan the full stencil and write one end.
The coordinate columns and the bond rows are allocated once and reused on the next frame of the same size.

A centre whose four bonds do not all lie between 2.30 Å and 3.35 Å does not open a second shell.

* Tetrahedral order

The tetrahedral order parameter q is the Chau–Hardwick / Errington–Debenedetti invariant of the four bonds (Errington and Debenedetti, /Nature/ 409, 318, 2001, [[https://doi.org/10.1038/35053024][10.1038/35053024]]).
A shell with q below 0.94 does not open the second shell.
That gate was chosen on the training seeds below.
A shell that does open and then measures q below 0.88 is liquid.

* Common-neighbour fingerprint

The second shell is the adaptive-cutoff common-neighbour signature of Maras, Trushin, Stukowski, Albe and Nordlund, /Computer Physics Communications/ 205, 13 (2016), Appendix A ([[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpc.2016.04.001][10.1016/j.cpc.2016.04.001]]).
The cutoff for a bond of length r is r(1+sqrt(2))/2.
The twelve neighbour distances are compared with that cutoff in one vector pass.

An exact cubic signature, twelve (4,2,1) patterns, is ice Ic.
An exact hexagonal signature, six (4,2,1) and six (4,2,2), is ice Ih.
A partial cubic signature with at least eight (4,2,1) patterns and q at least 0.97 is ice Ic.
A partial hexagonal signature with q at least 0.97 is ice Ih.
Six (4,2,1) patterns are not labelled here: on the training seeds that count is the liquid false positive, and the shell is passed to the fit instead.
An ideal shell, equal bonds and one second-shell radius, is named from the cosine of each second-shell bond with the other first-shell bonds and does not build this signature.
The ideal staggered cosine is 1/3 and the ideal eclipsed cosine is about 0.78.

* The fit

A shell is fitted only when it already has one crystal neighbour, q is at least 0.95, the four bonds differ by at most 15 percent, and the second-shell radius varies by at most a factor 1.6.
The residual is scale-free.
Both clouds are centred, the optimal rotation is a quaternion (Horn, /Journal of the Optical Society of America A/ 4, 629, 1987, [[https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAA.4.000629][10.1364/JOSAA.4.000629]]; Coutsias, Seok and Dill, /Journal of Computational Chemistry/ 25, 1849, 2004, [[https://doi.org/10.1002/jcc.20110][10.1002/jcc.20110]]), and the residual is divided by the template radius, which is the normalisation Larsen, Schmidt and Schiøtz use.
Zero is a perfect shape.
The ceiling is 0.12.

The bond-angle fingerprint names the one template the fit tries.
An arm whose cosine against the other bonds is above 0.90 is eclipsed.
Four eclipsed arms are liquid, except on the recovery pass, where that pattern is hexagonal.
The eclipsed arm is anchored to the eclipsed arm of the template.
The four-point orientation uses one Jacobi sweep and the seventeen-atom residual uses two.
A shell that is still far after a handful of correspondences is not crystal, and a shell with only one crystal neighbour stops earlier than a shell with two.
Hexagonal recovery is one pass over the atoms the first fit left unlabelled, and it tries the other templates.

After the labels are written, a crystal atom that has no crystal neighbour among its four bonds is returned to liquid.
The tests read the labels from the fit, before that filter.

* Cages, rings, and the other codes

The same in-tree matcher names a cage or a ring.
It is a 3×3 SVD with the permutation fixed by the bond graph.
The continuous shape measure of that overlay is the scaled residual of Pinsky and Avnir (/Inorganic Chemistry/ 37, 5575, 1998, [[https://doi.org/10.1021/ic9804925][10.1021/ic9804925]]) and of the Alvarez shape program (/Coordination Chemistry Reviews/ 249, 1693, 2005, [[https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccr.2005.03.031][10.1016/j.ccr.2005.03.031]]).
The shape program is not in the tree.

IRA (Gunde, Salles, Hémeryck and Martin-Samos, /Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling/ 61, 5446, 2021, [[https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567][10.1021/acs.jcim.1c00567]]) is an optional second matcher for the same templates.
SOFI, from the same group (/Journal of Chemical Physics/ 161, 062503, 2024, [[https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0215689][10.1063/5.0215689]]), names the point group.
spglib (Togo, Shinohara and Tanaka, /Science and Technology of Advanced Materials: Methods/ 4, 2384822, 2024, [[https://doi.org/10.1080/27660400.2024.2384822][10.1080/27660400.2024.2384822]]) names the space group.
CHILL+ (Nguyen and Molinero, /Journal of Physical Chemistry B/ 119, 9369, 2015, [[https://doi.org/10.1021/jp510289t][10.1021/jp510289t]]) is a different label and is not this shell.

* Calling it

C++, in ~symm~:

- ~classifyIce(cartesian, vectors, kind)~ writes one label per row.
  ~kind~ is ice Ih, ice Ic, ice Isd, or -1.
  The default ceiling is ~kShellRmsd~ (0.12).
- ~classifyIceArrays(x, y, z, n, lattice, kind)~ is the same call on three coordinate columns.
- ~best~, ~matchKernel~ and ~matchAll~ name one environment.
  Template ids are ice Ih, ice Ic, ice Isd, 5^12, 5^12 6^2, 5^12 6^4, ring-5 and ring-6.
- ~pointGroup~ needs SOFI.
  ~spaceGroup~ needs spglib.
  Either returns status 2 when that library was not linked.
- ~seams symmetry~ on the command line prints the space group, one shell overlay, and the label tally.

C, in ~seams_c_api.h~:

- ~seams_symmetry_classify~ and ~seams_symmetry_classify_tol~ take row-major coordinates and three lattice rows, and write the same labels.
- ~seams_symmetry_match~, ~seams_symmetry_template~, ~seams_symmetry_crystal~, ~seams_symmetry_point_group~, ~seams_symmetry_space_group~ and ~seams_symmetry_backend~ cover one cluster.

Python, ~bindings/python/symmetry.py~, loads ~libyodaLib~ through ctypes.
Set ~SEAMS_CORE_LIB~ to the built library.
~classify(cartesian, lattice, shell_rmsd=0.12)~ is the per-atom label.

* Build options

~with_ira~ is a feature option, ~auto~ by default.
When libira links, the matcher can use IRA and SOFI.
IRA is the Apache-2.0 option of its dual licence, and it is not required to build.

~with_csm~ is a feature option, ~disabled~ by default.
The continuous shape measure program is GPL-2.0.
It is an external executable (~csm_shape~ or ~shape~), it is not vendored, and it is not linked into ~libyodaLib~.
The Catch2 case that compares the in-tree measure with that program runs only when ~CSM_SHAPE~ is set.
~with_spglib~ stays ~auto~ and links libsymspg when it is present.

On GCC and Clang the shell translation unit is a separate static library built with ~-mavx2 -mfma~.
Other compilers compile it into ~libyodaLib~ with the ordinary flags.
A release build is ~-O3~.

* Held-out protocol

The frames are the steepest-descent quenches of three mW nucleation seeds, 91001, 92002 and 93003, each a 12×12×12 ice Ic cell that was melted, reseeded, and run at 220 K.
Seeds 91001 and 92002 are the training pair.
They chose the q gate and the ceiling 0.12.
Seed 93003 was not used to choose either, and it is the held-out frame.

Three methods label each quench with their own production rule: this kernel, CHILL+, and polyhedral template matching at an RMSD cutoff of 0.1 with cubic and hexagonal diamond enabled.
The majority truth is the template named by at least two of the three.
The persistent truth keeps an atom only when the majority template is the same on the 190 ps quench and the 200 ps quench.
A prediction is a true positive when the truth is crystal and the prediction names that same template, a false positive when the prediction is crystal and is not the truth, and a false negative when the truth is crystal and the prediction differs.

On the held-out quench the kernel at ceiling 0.12 is

| truth | true positive | false positive | false negative | precision | recall |
|-------+---------------+----------------+----------------+-----------+--------|
| majority | 328 | 6 | 63 | 0.9820 | 0.8389 |
| persistent | 319 | 15 | 47 | 0.9551 | 0.8716 |

Polyhedral template matching on the same two truths is majority precision 0.9635 and recall 0.8107 (317 true positives, 12 false positives, 74 false negatives), and persistent precision 0.9453 and recall 0.8497.
The kernel is ahead of template matching on both precision and recall.
Raising the ceiling to 0.125 on the training pair adds false positives as well as true positives, and an embedded-only relaxation of the ceiling did the same, so the production ceiling stays 0.12.
The held-out recall is the number above.
It was not pushed up by looking at seed 93003.
