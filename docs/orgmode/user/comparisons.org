#+TITLE: Head-to-head
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

Measured comparisons of d-SEAMS 2.14.0 with other structure codes.
Each timing table copies fields from the file named in the sentence
above it. The cage census is read out of the agreement text of the
cage file.

A time in the summary and cage tables is the median of =n= repeats.
The ratio is that file's =ratio= field: the other median divided by
the d-SEAMS median. In those two files every stored ratio is greater
than 1: the other median is the larger time. Where a table has an
agreement column, that column is the stored agreement text through
the first semicolon. The cage timing table's last column is the
cage name. The census under it is the rest of the agreement text.
Notes about serial calls are in the section prose.

* FindRings and the primitive census

The hexagon counts above are one check. The subset result is
another, and it is what the tests recompute.

On K2,3, d-SEAMS reports three primitive 4-rings and OVITO
FindRings reports two. ~tests/test_ovito_ring_counts.py~ builds the
graph so a 3.5 Å cutoff has six edges, and it requires three
d-SEAMS 4-rings, two FindRings 4-rings, and those six edges.
~tests/test_franzblau_extra.cpp~ requires the three primitive
4-rings and requires each of them to pass the Franzblau test. The
case name is "K2,3 has three primitive 4-rings".
[[file:../explanation/ovito-findrings.org][Primitive rings and OVITO FindRings]]
lists the three cycles.

On the 864-atom frame ~input/traj/interface_f0.lammpstrj~, cutoff
3.5 Å, the C++ test requires 1929 bonds and this primitive census:
142 of size 3, 188 of size 4, 377 of size 5, 1143 of size 6, 257 of
size 7, 54 of size 8, 52 of size 9, and 449 of size 10. It requires
every one of those rings to pass the Franzblau test. The sum of
those bins is 2662. The Python test loads the same trajectory,
requires the bond count to match on both sides, requires every
listed d-SEAMS ring to be primitive, and requires each FindRings
bin from size 4 through 10 to be at most the primitive bin.
~meson test ovito_ring_counts~ runs the Python test. ~meson test
franzblau_extra~ runs the C++ test.

* Per-atom q4 and q6

~tests/test_q4_q6_equal.py~ compares per-atom \(q_4\) and \(q_6\),
the Lechner-Dellago averages, and the mean of each, on three
frames. Ideal ice Ih is ~input/traj/ice1h.lammpstrj~. Ideal ice Ic
is a diamond cell with a 2.75 Å bond, four cells on a side. The
liquid is that cell plus Gaussian noise of 0.9 Å, seed 91001. The
neighbour rule in the test is the same in d-SEAMS, freud, and
pyscal3: a pair counts when its minimum-image distance is strictly
below 3.5 Å. The test runs at one thread and at eight.

pyscal3 is float64. Local \(q_l\), the Lechner-Dellago average, and
the mean of each over atoms that have a shell must agree to
\(10^{-10}\). freud stores \(q_l\) as float32. On the two crystals
the test requires the gap to stay within 8 units in the last place
of that float32 value. On the liquid the spherical-harmonic sum is
accumulated in float32, and the test requires the residual to stay
under \(2 \times 10^{-5}\).

An empty shell is 0 in d-SEAMS and NaN in freud and pyscal3. The
test requires that split. 0 is the empty accumulator.
[[file:howto/q4-q6.org][Local q4 and q6]] records why that 0 stays:
a NaN would enter every mean and every CHILL+ label.

The test does not call OVITO. Its opening statement is that OVITO
3.15.5 has no =SteinhardtParametersModifier=. OVITO 3.17.2 \(q_4\)
and \(q_6\) are timed in the 884736-molecule sitting named above,
in ~docs/records/explanation/performance-h2h-tables.rst~.
